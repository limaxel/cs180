# CS180 Project 4A - Image Warping and Mosaicing

I computed homographies from matching points, used them to rectify images, and stitched three sets of photos into mosaics. I also tried cylindrical projection for the wider scenes.

<!--more-->

# A.1 - Taking the pictures

I used three sets of overlapping photos from a building courtyard (The Gateway), an outdoor exercise area (Muscle Beach Venice), and a beach (Ocean Beach). The goal was to rotate the view from roughly the same camera position, although small movements and moving subjects can still cause alignment problems. The source photos are shown alongside each mosaic below.

# A.2 - Recovering homographies

<p>A homography maps a point in one image to a point in another using \(\mathbf{p}'\sim H\mathbf{p}\). I used SIFT to propose matching points for the mosaics, filtered the matches with my own RANSAC implementation, and saved the selected correspondences. I then computed \(H\) using least squares rather than a built-in homography solver.</p>

<p>With \(h_{33}=1\), each correspondence \((x,y)\mapsto(u,v)\) gives two equations.</p>

<div class="p4-equation">
\[
\begin{bmatrix}
x & y & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & -ux & -uy \\
0 & 0 & 0 & x & y & 1 & -vx & -vy
\end{bmatrix}
\mathbf{h} =
\begin{bmatrix}u\\v\end{bmatrix}
\]
</div>

I used more than four correspondences and normalized the points before solving. The fitted matrix below is for the first building pair.

# A.3 - Image rectification

<p>I wrote nearest-neighbor and bilinear samplers and used inverse warping. Instead of sending each input pixel forward and leaving gaps, I mapped every output pixel back to its source position with \(H^{-1}\).</p>

Nearest neighbor takes the closest pixel. Bilinear interpolation combines the four surrounding pixels using their fractional distances. I used the same homography for both methods so only the sampling changed.

I selected the four corners of the sign and mapped them to a rectangle. The target proportions use an approximate measurement of 43 by 71 cm from the iPhone Measure app.

# A.4 - Image mosaics

I warped the photos onto a common canvas, keeping a middle frame as the reference. Simply overwriting one image with another left obvious seams. I used distance-weighted feathering so that pixels near an image boundary contributed less to the overlap.

The basic mosaics use my bilinear inverse warp and feathering. I also tried exposure correction and a two-band seam blend where moving objects caused noticeable ghosting.

## Building courtyard (The Gateway)

This was a three-image mosaic. My first attempt used a reference image at one end of the sweep, which stretched the opposite side quite badly. Using the middle frame worked better.

# A.5 - Cylindrical projection

For the extra part, I tried projecting the mosaics onto a cylinder instead of a plane. The planar versions stretched the ends of the wider scenes. I used an approximate focal length for each photo set and sampled directly from the original images, rather than rewarping an already stitched mosaic.

<p>For horizontal cylindrical position \(u\), I used \(\theta=(u-c_x)/f\) to find the corresponding viewing angle. I then mapped that ray back through the inverse homography to sample each photo.</p>
